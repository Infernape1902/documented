# 1. Review: Isotopes

## Definition

> Isotopes are atoms of the same element that have different numbers of neutrons.

Examples:

| Isotope | Protons | Neutrons |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Carbon-12 | 6 | 6 |
| Carbon-13 | 6 | 7 |
| Carbon-14 | 6 | 8 |

Important:

- Same number of protons
- Different number of neutrons
- Different atomic masses

---

## Why Isotopes Matter

Different isotopes have:

- Different masses
- Different abundances in nature
- Different stability

Examples:

| Isotope | Stability |
|----------|----------|
| Carbon-12 | Stable |
| Carbon-13 | Stable |
| Carbon-14 | Radioactive |

---

# 2. Relative Abundance

## Definition

> The percentage of atoms of a specific isotope found naturally.

Example: Chlorine

| Isotope | Relative Abundance |
|----------|----------|
| Cl-35 | 75.76% |
| Cl-37 | 24.24% |

Meaning:

Out of 100 chlorine atoms:

- ~76 are Cl-35
- ~24 are Cl-37

---

## Important Rule

The sum of all isotope abundances must equal:

```text
100%
```

Example:

```text
75.76% + 24.24%

= 100%
```

---

# 3. Average Atomic Mass

The atomic mass shown on the periodic table is a weighted average.

Formula:

```text
Average Atomic Mass

= Σ(Relative Abundance × Atomic Mass)
```

---

## Example: Chlorine

```text
(0.7576 × 34.97)

+

(0.2424 × 36.97)

= 35.45 u
```

Therefore:

```text
Atomic Mass of Cl ≈ 35.45 u
```

---

# 4. What is Mass Spectrometry?

## Definition

> Mass spectrometry is an experimental technique used to determine the masses and relative abundances of atoms or molecules.

Instrument:

```text
Mass Spectrometer
```

---

## Main Purpose

Mass spectrometry allows us to determine:

- Isotopes present
- Relative abundance
- Atomic masses
- Molecular masses

---

# 5. How a Mass Spectrometer Works

## Step 1: Vaporization

Sample enters the instrument.

```text
Sample
↓
Gas phase
```

---

## Step 2: Ionization

High-energy electrons remove electrons from atoms.

Example:

```text
Na → Na+
```

Result:

Positive ions are produced.

---

## Step 3: Acceleration

Electric field accelerates ions.

```text
Positive ions
↓
Accelerated forward
```

---

## Step 4: Deflection

Magnetic field bends ion paths.

Important:

- Lighter ions bend more
- Heavier ions bend less

---

## Step 5: Detection

Detector records:

- Number of ions
- Mass-to-charge ratio

---

# 6. Mass-to-Charge Ratio (m/z)

One of the most important concepts.

Formula:

```text
m/z
```

Where:

- m = mass
- z = charge

---

## Example 1

Ion:

```text
35Cl+
```

Mass:

```text
35
```

Charge:

```text
+1
```

Therefore:

```text
m/z = 35
```

---

## Example 2

Ion:

```text
63Cu2+
```

Mass:

```text
63
```

Charge:

```text
2
```

Therefore:

```text
m/z = 31.5
```

---

# 7. Mass Spectrum

A mass spectrum is the graph produced by a mass spectrometer.

---

## Axes

### X-axis

```text
m/z
```

Mass-to-charge ratio

---

### Y-axis

```text
Relative abundance
```

Number of ions detected

---

## Interpretation

Peak position:

```text
Mass
```

Peak height:

```text
Abundance
```

---

# 8. Example: Chlorine Spectrum

Natural chlorine contains:

```text
Cl-35
Cl-37
```

Expected spectrum:

Peak at:

```text
35
```

and

```text
37
```

---

Peak heights:

Approximately

```text
76%
```

and

```text
24%
```

because those are the natural abundances.

---

# 9. Identifying an Element from a Spectrum

Example:

Observed peaks:

```text
24
25
26
```

Possible element:

```text
Magnesium
```

because naturally occurring magnesium contains:

```text
24Mg
25Mg
26Mg
```

---

## Strategy

Step 1:

Look at peak positions.

```text
Mass values
```

---

Step 2:

Look at peak heights.

```text
Relative abundances
```

---

Step 3:

Compare with known isotope data.
